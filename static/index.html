asterism

Upload a photo of the night sky — get back a labelled map of what you were looking at.

What is this?

Upload a photo of the night sky and asterism tells you what you were looking at: it names the stars, draws the constellations, and marks the Moon and planets in your frame.

It works out where your camera was pointing from the star pattern alone — no location, no compass, no account.

Works best with an ordinary phone photo of the night sky. Handheld and a little hazy is fine; the more stars you can see, the better it does.

Taking a photo the solver can read

The solver needs stars in the pixels, not just in the sky. A few seconds of exposure makes more difference than anything else.

  • Use night mode. iPhone: Night mode comes on by itself in the dark (the yellow moon icon) — tap it and drag the slider to the longest time it offers. Pixel: Night Sight, which switches to Astrophotography on its own once the phone is still. Samsung: Night mode.
  • Hold still — any way you can. A tripod is ideal, but a phone leaned against a wall, laid flat on a car roof, wedged in a shoe, or pressed into a beanbag or a bag of rice works too. Use the timer or a volume button so the tap doesn't shake it.
  • Zoom out, not in. The main lens or the ultrawide, with no digital zoom: more sky in the frame means more stars to match.
  • Get away from lights. A streetlight in the frame flattens the sky. Haze, suburbs, and a bit of glow are fine — a dark sky helps, but the long exposure matters more.
  • Mind the Moon and the clouds. A bright Moon or thin cloud washes out everything fainter. Another hour, or another night.
  • Upload the original. Screenshots, edits, and photos passed through a messaging app lose the camera's notes about when and how the shot was taken — the solver uses those to explain what it sees.
Uploads are public, and deleted after 24 hours unless you keep them.

Anyone with the result link can view your photo and its labels, and successful solves appear in the "recently solved" feed on this page. GPS metadata is removed from the stored photo (uploads with location data we can't remove are rejected), coordinates shown in results are rounded to city scale, and everything is deleted after 24 hours — unless you choose to keep your solve on the site from its result page, or it is one of the occasional solves kept on as an example of what the site does. Photos are also sent to an AI service (Anthropic) to write the short description of what they show.
