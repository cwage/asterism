asterism

Point your phone at the night sky. Find out what you saw.

?

Upload a photo of the night sky — even a hazy, handheld phone shot — and asterism plate-solves it: the star pattern in your pixels is matched against a map of the entire sky (astrometry.net) to work out exactly where the camera was pointing, with no location or orientation data needed.

It then names the stars it can see, draws the constellation figures, circles the naked-eye deep-sky objects in frame (the Pleiades, the Orion Nebula, Andromeda), and — when the photo carries a timestamp — marks the Moon and planets computed for that instant. Labels it can't verify against an actual dot in your photo (clouds, usually) are drawn dashed.

No account, no app: your phone camera is the instrument. Source on GitHub.

Uploads are public: anyone with the result link can view your photo and its labels, and successful solves appear in the "recently solved" feed on this page. GPS metadata is removed from the stored photo (uploads with location data we can't remove are rejected), coordinates shown in results are rounded to city scale, and everything is deleted after 24 hours.
